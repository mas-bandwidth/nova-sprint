## Score: 8/10

I walked the tool the way a cold AI would: built the binary from a bare clone, ran `help` and `help <verb>` for all 40 verbs, and fired every safe verb against an empty machine. The engine is the best cold-work surface I have ever met — a complete self-describing help index, one-line refusals that each name their fix, and a three-way exit code that never lies under test. It is not 10 because a cold AI cannot actually start: the bare command points at a credential wall before it points at help, there is no throwaway store to practice the verbs on, and the docs have no entry point. Those are onboarding gaps, not engine gaps, and I also found one real contract defect — the `up` verb writes its refusal to a different stream than every other verb — which a harness that reads one stream will miss entirely.

## What is good

- `nova-sprint help` is a complete, self-contained contract. Its header states the exit-code contract word for word ("Exit 0: done, the last line says next. Exit 1: refused, one line naming the fix. Exit 2: bad usage, or the store, bus or an edge failed"), then groups all 40 verbs under `start:` / `work:` / `coordinate:` / `operate:` with each verb's role, its `refuses when`, and its `next`, and ends with `next: nova-sprint next --as <you>`. I learned the whole surface from this one command and never opened a doc to learn a verb.
- `nova-sprint help take` is the exact shape an AI needs: `who`, `up` ("the store up makes at step 2"), `refuses when (and the fix it names)`, `next`. The pattern "refuses when (and the fix it names)" is the tool's one governing idea and it is held on all 40 verbs I ran.
- The refusal is never silent and never empty. `nova-sprint check` on this cold machine printed three `CHECK RED` lines (nova-fleet's `CHECK MISSING ansible-playbook ...`, the missing sprint credential, `nova-bus who: missing NOVA_BUS_REDIS (host:port)`), each naming its own fix, before a final `SPRINT REFUSED check: 3 lines are red; the first: ...`. The subprocess stderr is embedded verbatim, so I trust the diagnosis instead of guessing.
- The exit-code contract held under test. `version` -> 0 with `next: nova-sprint help`; `up` with no `--as` -> 1 with "up names its coordinator: nova-sprint up --as <you>"; `queue`/`show` with no name -> 1 with "pass --as <you>, or set NOVA_SPRINT_AS=<you>"; `take`/`finish`/`hook` without a bus -> 2 with "nova-bus who failed ... check NOVA_BUS_REDIS, then nova-bus who"; an unknown verb -> 2 with "unknown verb frobnicate: nova-sprint help". Every refusal names its fix; I saw no silent zero.
- `help playbook worker` and `help playbook coordinator` embed the full playbooks in the binary (`docs/playbooks.go`), so a worker never has to find a file to know its rules. `docs/PLAYBOOK-WORKER.md` "The refusals you will meet" (lines 257-292) is a lookup table mapping each refusal a worker meets to its exact repair.
- `docs/CORRECT-USE.md` "The one rule" (line 13) and the chain table (lines 39-51) are the whole enforcement philosophy in one page: the tool enforces order instead of trusting an LLM to remember it, and every successful verb ends with `next:`.
- The deal/brief form is enough to work cold. My own card arrived as a brief carrying tier, PATHS, the done-when command and the finish verb, and the harness's `JOB.md` carried the rest; `take`/`finish`/`read`/`report`/`release` cover every honest outcome (done, failed, harness fault, missing-route, going away), so I never had to invent an outcome the tool does not know.

## What gets it to 10

1. **A throwaway store verb.** `store.NewFake` and `store.NewFakeFrom` (`store/fake.go`, `store/fakefrom.go`) are test-only seams; the CLI opens only the real store via `store.Open(ctx, NOVA_PG_DSN, NOVA_SPRINT_PG_PASSWORD)` in `cmd/nova-sprint/open.go:127`, with no `--store` flag or env. Add `nova-sprint demo` (or `--store fake`). Done when: from a bare `git clone`, `demo` then `take`/`finish`/`read`/`judge`/`next` run against the in-memory store and print their real next-lines with no PostgreSQL, Redis, Tailscale or nova-secrets.
2. **Fix what the bare binary prints first.** `nova-sprint` with no arguments runs `next` and prints `NEXT this process has no sprint credential` then `next: run under nova-secrets exec --only NOVA_SPRINT_PG_PASSWORD,NOVA_BUS_PASSWORD -- nova-sprint ...` (exit 0). That is a wall naming a tool a cold machine does not have, and it never says "read help" — yet `docs/CORRECT-USE.md` lines 29-33 claim the bare command "tells the AI what to do". Make the claim true: point at the reading path first (`next: nova-sprint help`, then the worker playbook) and only mention the credential after that pointer. Done when: the bare command's first `next:` line names a readable verb, and the credential/fleet requirement appears only after the AI is pointed at help.
3. **One refusal stream.** The `up` verb's setup wrapper writes its `SPRINT REFUSED up:` lines to stderr (`cmd/nova-sprint/up_wrapper.go:59-100`), while every other verb writes its refusal to stdout (`cmd/nova-sprint/main.go:59`, `executeVerb`). I reproduced it: `up --as hulk` exits 1 with 0 bytes on stdout and the whole refusal on stderr, whereas `take foo@1 --as hulk` exits 2 with the whole refusal on stdout. Done when: every refusal, success and `next:` line goes to stdout, and the exit-code contract in `help` and `CORRECT-USE.md` names the stream, so a harness reading one stream never sees an empty exit 1.
4. **A one-page quickstart.** `docs/SETUP.md` opens by admitting it is "what the setup must be", not "the walkthrough an AI runs" (lines 3-4). Add `docs/QUICKSTART.md` (install nova-tools -> `nova-fleet up` -> `nova-sprint up` -> `hook` -> `next` -> `take` -> `finish`), one line per step with its first output and first refusal, and point `help` and the top of `docs/README.md` at it. Done when: a cold AI follows only that page plus `nova-sprint help` and reaches a working `take` without opening the other ~8,000 lines.
5. **Per-verb doc pointers.** The 8,860 lines of docs carry no "read this when" signal, so an AI reads layer history to learn how to use a verb. Add a `docs:` line to `help <verb>` naming the one file and section that governs that verb (e.g. `help take` -> `docs: PLAYBOOK-WORKER.md "Work a card"`). Done when: every `help <verb>` names its governing section, and `help` names QUICKSTART first.
6. **Fix the doubled refusal on a bad playbook name.** `help playbook bogus` prints two concatenated fixes, `...: nova-sprint help playbook worker: nova-sprint help help`, which is one refusal that names two different commands. Done when: a bad playbook name yields one `SPRINT REFUSED help: the playbooks are worker and coordinator: nova-sprint help playbook worker`.

## Where I would be lost cold

1. **The bare binary points at a tool I do not have.** `nova-sprint` (no args) -> "this process has no sprint credential: run under nova-secrets exec ...", and `nova-secrets` is not installed because nova-tools is not set up. Verb: `nova-sprint` / `next`. The first thing the tool tells me to run depends on infrastructure I have not been told to install, and the install order (fleet before secrets before store) lives only in `docs/SETUP.md` prose, not in the tool.
2. **There is no way to try the verbs safely.** `docs/SETUP.md` step 1 requires the full nova-tools fleet (Tailscale, Redis, PostgreSQL, Ansible) before anything but `help`/`version`/`check` acts; the in-memory store is `store/fake.go`, a test-only seam, so "try the verbs against a throwaway store" has no CLI. A cold AI cannot practice `take`/`finish`/`read` without standing up a fleet it cannot yet stand up.
3. **The docs have no entry point.** `docs/README.md` is a layer-by-layer lock history and design log, not a "how do I use this"; the actual instructions are split across `SETUP.md`, `PLAYBOOK-WORKER.md`, `PLAYBOOK-COORDINATOR.md` and `CORRECT-USE.md`, and the tool's own `help` index — which is the best entry and the token-cheapest — is only reached if I already know to run it.

## Tokens and time

- The refusal-stream defect cost the most to pin down. Because the tool's output is merged in a harness, I had to rerun `up` with stdout and stderr redirected to separate files before I could see the refusal was on stderr and nowhere on stdout; that is exactly the failure a harness that reads only stdout would hit silently.
- Reconstructing the cold-start order from three separate refusals (`up` on secrets, acting verbs on `NOVA_BUS_REDIS`, `next` on the credential) plus `docs/SETUP.md` prose, instead of reading one ordered list. The order exists nowhere in one place.
- Docs volume. To reach a working mental model I read `SETUP.md` (90), `PLAYBOOK-WORKER.md` (312), `CORRECT-USE.md` (287) and `docs/README.md` (223) — about 900 of 8,860 lines — because no single page is the walkthrough. The `help` index would have saved most of it if I had known to start there.
- Cheap, in contrast: `nova-sprint help` (one command = the whole 40-verb surface and the exit contract) and `help <verb>` (who/up/refuses/next per verb). These are the token-efficient parts, and they are the parts I actually relied on.
