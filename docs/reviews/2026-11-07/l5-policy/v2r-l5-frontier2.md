# Cold review: layer 5, policy (v2r-l5-frontier2, rowan, Fable 5.1)

Reviewed at 43d849e2 on v2-stitch. Read: docs/LAYER-5-POLICY.md, docs/ENFORCED.md (L5 rows), every file of policy/ (34 files, 8,455 lines with tests), tla/Policy.tla, tla/PolicyTrace.tla, the ten Policy witnesses, and the consumers of the answers in run/, steps/, verbs/ and roster/ where a promise of this layer is kept or broken on the other side of the seam. Ran: `go build ./... && go vet ./...` (clean); `go test -count=1 ./policy/` (ok, 2.6 s: the functional, timing and 2,000-file fuzz tests skip without PostgreSQL, NOVA_LOAD and NOVA_POLICY_FUZZ); one throwaway test to measure the 4 MiB answer cap (pasted under defect 3, deleted before the commit). Extra weight, as asked, on what a policy can get wrong silently and on the catalog, prices and width answers.

## Verdict

The Starlark embedding, the validator and the version/fallback machinery are tight and well tested inside the package: every domain refusal has a row with its exact text, the deadline is on a fake clock, the evaluator child is bounded, and the model's actions match the engine's `race`. The layer is not sound at its seam. The single worst thing: a large part of what a policy says is validated, accepted, named in the tick's receipt, and then never read. `prices()` reaches no consumer at all; `width().ahead` is read by no step; five of the twelve required `limits()` keys (`escalate_after`, `frontier_after`, `flap_limit`, `idle_floor`, `land_target`) are dropped by run and steps uses its own Go literals; and when the built-in answers a tick (version 0) steps ignores every answer by ruling. The coordinator can change these rules, see `POLICY STORED`, see `answered_by`, and nothing changes: exactly the silent failure the brief asked me to weigh. Beside that, run's `NoDeal` gate makes the fallback deal nothing while the doc and `tla/Policy.tla` still say the previous version or the built-in answers the tick and may deal, so the model's `Tick` and the trace walk describe a rule the binary no longer runs; and the 4 MiB answer cap is a cliff at about 17,000 ready producers where every version fails alike and the sprint stops dealing with a judgement that blames the policy.

## Defects

1. **serious** — `limits()` steering keys are required, validated, and discarded. `policy/validate.go:178-179` requires `escalate_after`, `frontier_after`, `flap_limit`, `idle_floor`, `land_target` (`checkLimits` at 184-213 refuses a file without them, and `policy/default.star:62-68` supplies them). `run/asker.go:119-121` copies only `Bounces, Faults, SameFinding, ReportsPerTick, RouteRestWindow, RouteRestAfter` into `steps.Limits` (`steps/asker.go:108-113` has no field for the five). Steps reads `Engine.FrontierAfter`/`Engine.EscalateAfter` at `steps/deal.go:302-304`, `Engine.FlapLimit` at `steps/limits.go:48`, `Engine.LandTarget` at `steps/judge.go:206,262` and `Engine.IdleFloor` at `steps/judge.go:268`, all from the literals at `steps/asker.go:154-156`. Why it matters: the doc's question table (`docs/LAYER-5-POLICY.md:102`, "layer 3's steering numbers (round 5)") and the boundary row for escalation promise these are policy's; a coordinator who sets `escalate_after: 3` gets `POLICY STORED`, a clean `check`, and the sprint keeps escalating at 2, with no refusal and no judgement. Repair: carry the five through `stepsAnswers` into `steps.Limits` and read them from the oracle, or drop them from `limitKeys` and the doc until layer 3 reads them.

2. **serious** — `prices()` has no consumer. `policy/answer.go:131-132` asks it every tick and `policy/validate.go:327-356` validates it; `Answers.Prices` (`policy/policy.go:83`) is read by nothing outside the package: `grep -rn Prices --include='*.go' . | grep -v _test | grep -v '^./policy/'` returns only `card/*` and `edge/*` lines about the worker's `Priced` flag, and `dash/shim.go:332-333` counts `unpriced` from that flag. The boundary row `docs/LAYER-5-POLICY.md:71` ("cost per token per model, for the dashboard | `prices`") is a promise nothing keeps: a coordinator who prices a model sees every run stay unpriced. There is also no vocabulary for the key: the table says "model name", while a consumer's model is written as the bench's `provider/model` (`policy/catalog.go:81-95` `ForTier`, `verbs/apply.go:101`), so even once wired a price keyed `deepseek-flash` would miss a run recorded as `deepseek/deepseek-flash`. Repair: either wire `Prices` into the dashboard's cost (keyed by the recorded `provider/model`, with the catalog check refusing a price for a model the catalog does not list) or remove the question from `Arity` and the doc; validating a dead answer is the worst of both.

3. **serious** — the 4 MiB answer cap fails every version alike, so the fallback cannot help and the sprint stops dealing at a snapshot size, not a policy fault. `policy/tick.go:178-180` fails the version when the tally passes `maxTickBytes` (`policy/engine.go:46-48`, 4 MiB); the cost is per ask (`entryBytes` 96 at `policy/tick.go:280`, plus `weight` at 303-321), so it grows with the questions, which every version is asked alike. Measured with a throwaway test (ready producers over 50 rows of width 2, the default policy, `testEngine()`):

   ```
   producers=5000  version=1  tallied_bytes=1262056 failures=0
   producers=10000 version=1  tallied_bytes=2502056 failures=0
   producers=15000 version=1  tallied_bytes=3742056 failures=0
   producers=20000 version=-1 failures=2 v1: the tick's answers are over 4194304 bytes; default: the tick's answers are over 4194304 bytes
   ```

   248 bytes per ready producer (order 152, rows 96), so the cliff is at about 16,900 ready producers. Past it `Answers.Version` is -1 every tick, `policy/answer.go:82-90` opens a `policy:v<n>` and a `policy:v0` judgement whose command is `nova-sprint policy check v<n>`; `check` on the same snapshot prints the same cap message for every answer past it (`policy/tick.go:151` goes on past a refusal when `all` is set), so the fix text sends the coordinator to a policy change that cannot help, and run deals nothing (`run/asker.go:100-102`). The doc says only "a version over the cap fails" (`docs/LAYER-5-POLICY.md:614-616`). Why it matters: guarantee 4 (`docs/LAYER-5-POLICY.md:293-295`, "every tick is answered by the active, the previous or the built-in version") is broken by the input's size, with a judgement that names the wrong cause. Repair: make the cap a refusal of the *tick* (Version -1, one failure of no version, like the stale-seq refusal at `policy/answer.go:57-59`) with a message naming the producer count, or count only answer weight (not the 96 per entry) and raise the cap to what 5,000 producers times a safety factor needs; and say the bound in the doc.

4. **serious** — policy's candidate set is wider than steps', so an in-domain `rows` answer (the default's included) is refused by steps and replaced by the engine's ranking, with a judgement blaming the policy. `policy/inputs.go:137-141` admits a row that is up, not held, serves the tier, and is not the author; `steps/tick.go:216-223` also excludes a row that is resting (`t.rested`), claimed by a blocker this tick (`t.unclaimed`), does not serve every route the card declares (`t.in.Roster.ServesRoutes(n, routes)`, from `card.NeedsRoutes(c.Brief)`), and for a read one holding a live read of the head (`readingOn`, 243). The default's `rows` returns every candidate (`policy/default.star:33-39`, `policy/prefer.go:55-80`), so for any card with a `Routes:` line, or on any tick with a resting or claimed row, the answer names a row steps did not offer; `steps/asker.go:229-247` then refuses the whole answer (`o.refuse("rows")`, 240) and `steps/judge.go:148-149` opens `policy v<n> failed question rows: ; the default answered this tick: run nova-sprint policy check` (the message is empty since `Failed["rows"]` is not set), and `check` reports nothing wrong. Policy cannot even compute the routes: `policy/evaluator.go:152-160` strips `Brief` before the child sees the snapshot. `run/asker.go:14-18` says "policy computes the facts itself from the same snapshot and roster, so the two never disagree about a card or a row"; they disagree on four conditions. The joint check's "could take it" (`policy/answer.go:176-181`) overcounts for the same reason. Repair: one candidate rule, computed once in Go from the snapshot before `unworded` (routes, rest, claims and live reads folded into `candidateSet`, with rest and claims passed in as tick inputs), and steps' oracle checking names against *that* list rather than recomputing a narrower one.

5. **serious** — the built-in's answers are never used by steps, so the fallback the layer promises is a receipt line. `steps/asker.go:181-183` (`if o.a.Version == 0 { return false }`) makes every question unusable when version 0 answered, by the layer 3 ruling at `docs/LAYER-3-STEPS.md:473` ("no policy (version 0) is every default and no judgement"); steps then takes its own `Engine` numbers (`steps/asker.go:154-160`), a second copy of `default.star`'s values. `docs/LAYER-5-POLICY.md:235-237` and guarantee 4 at 293-295 say the built-in answers the tick; `docs/LAYER-5-POLICY.md:41` ("answers every snapshot the suite builds") and `TestDefaultAnswersEverySnapshot` test answers nobody reads. Two sources of the same numbers will drift (`steps/asker.go:159` `Landing{BackedUp: 2, Flowing: 1, CIWait: 600}` and `Timeouts` at 158 already lack `tick` and `consumer`). Repair: decide one of two: steps reads version 0's answers like any other (then delete `Engine`'s duplicates), or the layer 5 doc, `Versions.tried` (`policy/answer.go:27-36`) and the model drop the built-in from the fallback order and `up`/first `set` remain the only way its numbers run.

6. **serious** — `width().ahead` is required by the validator and read by no one. `policy/validate.go:161-175` refuses a `width` answer without `ahead` ("key ahead is missing", `policy/validate_test.go:56`) and bounds it at 4 x width; `steps/asker.go:93-98` says "policy's 'ahead' is read by no step since" and `WidthAnswer` has only `Work`; `run/asker.go:112-114` copies `Work` alone. The question table (`docs/LAYER-5-POLICY.md:101`) still describes `ahead` as policy's (PRIORITY D21). A coordinator must supply a number that does nothing and is refused if absent. Repair: drop `ahead` from `checkWidth`, `Width`, `default.star:53` and the table (the stitch already ruled "no deal-ahead for a worker", `docs/LAYER-5-POLICY.md:998-1004`).

7. **minor** — the default's `prefer` judges "room" by nova-config's width, not by the policy's own `width` answer. `policy/prefer.go:101-103` returns `working < width` where `width` is the candidate's configured width (`policy/inputs.go:199-203`); a policy that lowers `width().work` below the configured width and still calls `prefer` puts a row with no room by its own rule first. The default is consistent only because its `width` returns `row.width`. Repair: pass the tick's `Width.Work` to the candidate struct as `room` (or `work`) and let `prefer` use it; or say in the doc that `prefer` ranks by configured width.

8. **minor** — `checkPrices` accepts an empty model name and any name at all. `policy/validate.go:333-353` only requires a string key; `""` is stored as a price, and a misspelt model is accepted with no cross-check against `catalog()`'s names (`policy/catalog.go:128-162` has them). With defect 2 fixed this is the silent case: a priced model that never matches a run. Repair: refuse `""`, and refuse a price whose key is not a catalog model in `provider/model` form.

9. **minor** — the deal-time `ci_wait` has two "absent" encodings. `policy/validate.go:232-245` refuses `"ci_wait": 0` ("outside 1..86400", `policy/validate_test.go:83`) but turns an absent key into `CIWait: 0`, which `run/edges.go:133-135` reads as "use the engine's 10 minutes". An explicit 0 and an absent key mean the same thing and one is refused. Repair: either make the key required (the default supplies it) or accept 0 as "the engine's".

10. **minor** — a unit test reads the wall clock and runs a 5 s real timer. `policy/fuzz_break_test.go:458-472` `TestFuzzPolicyReplay` is ungated (no `breakTiming`, no `NOVA_POLICY_FUZZ`) and runs `runOne`, whose `select` at 384 races a `time.After(fuzzWallTime)`; its `t.Logf` timings at 468-470 use `time.Now`. It passed here in the ordinary `go test ./policy/` run. The doc's "Unit, package policy, no time, no sockets" (`docs/LAYER-5-POLICY.md:330`) and the standing rule say this is a functional test. Likewise `policy/functional_test.go:37` opens a TCP listener and starts PostgreSQL whenever `initdb` is on PATH (84), gated by the machine, not by a tier flag, inside the unit package. Repair: gate `TestFuzzPolicyReplay` with `breakTiming` or the fuzz env, and gate the PostgreSQL suite by a `NOVA_PG`-style flag rather than PATH.

11. **minor** — `Set` walks every stored version three times. `policy/verbs.go:93` (`sameBody`), 99 (`setWrites` → `versions`), 112 (`sameBody` again), each a loop of `st.Policy(ctx, v)` for v = 1.. (`policy/verbs.go:290-301`); `Up` and `Activate` once each. On PostgreSQL that is 3n round trips per `set`. Repair: read the versions once per verb and pass the slice down.

12. **nit** — two ticks compiling one version at once lose the answer cache for that version. `policy/engine.go:117-134` compiles outside the lock and the last `p` wins the map; `commit` (146-156) sets `p.answers` on the earlier `p`, which the map no longer holds, so the next tick is cold once. Harmless; worth a comment or a double-checked store.

13. **nit** — `Check` of the argument `0` or `v0` is read as a file name. `policy/show.go:68-74` `versionArg` returns ok only for n > 0, so `nova-sprint policy check 0` refuses "cannot read 0". `default` is the spelling; say so in the refusal.

## Model vs code

- `tla/Policy.tla:127-139` `Tick`: a deal (`card' = "working"`) needs only `Pick # NoAnswer` (132), so the model lets the previous version or `Default` deal. The binary never does: `run/asker.go:85` deals only when the active version answered. `NoAnswerStartsNothing` (161-162) is therefore weaker than the code's rule (`answeredBy # active => no deal`), and the model has no state for "no version active" at all (`Init` has `active = 1`), while run's `NoDeal` has that case. The trace walk encodes the model's rule, not run's: `policy/trace_test.go:160` deals when `a.Version != -1`. So `TestTraceWalk` (ENFORCED L5-14) binds the model to a walk, not to the binary's deal rule. Repair: either add `active \notin breaks` to the deal guard in `Tick` (and `NoAnswerStartsNothing` becomes `answeredBy # active => no deal`), or move the gate out of run so the fallback deals as the model says; then re-cut the walk and the `PolicyNoAnswerDeals` witness.
- `tla/Policy.tla:110-114` `Pick` and `Failing` (119-122) model `breaks` as a property of a version on a snapshot. The code has two failures of *the tick*, not of a version: the stale-seq refusal (`policy/answer.go:57-59`, Version -1, no judgement) and the answer-size cap (`policy/tick.go:178-180`, every version fails identically). Neither is a `Drift` the model can express (a `Drift` putting all of `Usable` in `breaks` opens judgements; the stale-seq case opens none). Minor: the model should say which code paths it abstracts as "all break" and the trace walk should hit the stale-seq refusal once.
- `tla/Policy.tla:93` `Activate` answers `judgements[Default]`; `policy/verbs.go:207` matches. `Set` with none active (`policy/verbs.go:121-136`) activates and answers `policy:v0`; the model has no such step (it starts with `active = 1`). Noted in the doc at 702-705 for `up`; the first-`set` activation is a second unmodelled path to `active`.
- `policy/prefer.go` and the deadline/evaluator are outside the model by design; the trace check's `breaks` (`policy/trace.go:81-86`) is computed by `checkRun` on a sibling engine, so the deadline is in it. Fine.
- The ten witnesses each flip one conjunct of `Policy.tla` and are otherwise byte-identical to it (checked `PolicyTickStalls` and `PolicyJudgementEveryTick` line by line); `PolicyTrace.tla` adds nothing. Fine.

## Simplifications

- Delete what nothing reads: `prices` (defect 2) or wire it; `ahead` (defect 6); the five steering keys from `limitKeys` or wire them (defect 1). That removes about 60 lines of validator and tests and three rows of the question table.
- One validation, one default. `steps/asker.go:229-343` re-checks every domain the policy validator already checked (`rows` membership, `reads` 1..5, `verdict` with a read that said so, `width` 0..width, `landing` ranges, `limitsOK`, `timeoutsOK`) and `steps/asker.go:154-160` restates `default.star`'s numbers. If steps trusts `policy.Answers` as the doc says ("the engine checks the answer against its domain before any move uses it", `docs/LAYER-5-POLICY.md:9-10`), about 120 lines of steps and the drift between the two copies go. The remaining steps-side check should be the one thing policy cannot know (defect 4's candidate rule), and it should be one function both call.
- `policy/show.go:178-190` `reasons` and `policy/verbs.go:290-301` `versions` walk the log or probe `Policy(v)` in a loop for `show`, `list`, `set`, `up`, `activate`, `diff`. A `Reason` column on the policy row (layer 1) or a `Policies(ctx)` read would remove both walkers.
- `policy/diff.go` (130 lines, Hirschberg) exists to diff two bodies of at most 64 KiB. `policy show a > a.star; policy show b > b.star; diff` is what the refusal text at `policy/diff.go:44-45` already tells the coordinator to do above 10,000 lines. The doc (762) names this as droppable; I agree.
- `policy/validate.go:216-225` `fraction` accepts an int or a float in (0, 1]; the only int in range is 1. Accepting floats only is simpler and the refusal text ("not a number") stays true.

Defects 1, 2 and 6 would each fail it.

Defect 4 fails it today.
- The 4 MiB cliff: a test at the scale target (5,000 producers) asserting the tally is under half the cap, and one above the cliff asserting the failure is a tick refusal that names the cause, not a per-version judgement with `policy check` as its fix (defect 3).

## Questions for the coordinator

- Is the built-in meant to answer ticks at all now? Layer 3's ruling (`docs/LAYER-3-STEPS.md:473`) and run's `NoDeal` say no; this layer's doc, model, `Versions.tried` and five witnesses say yes. Which document is canon decides defects 3 and 5 and the model change.
- Who owns the seam: should `run/asker.go:stepsAnswers` be a layer 5 function (`policy.Answers` already is the shape, steps could take it as is), so that a key added to a question cannot be dropped on the way?
- `prices`: wire or delete? If wire, the key vocabulary must be the recorded `provider/model`, and layer 1's `priced` flag means the worker priced the run itself; is policy's price a replacement for that or a fallback for unpriced runs?
- The routes a card declares live in its brief, which the evaluator strips for size (rowan2's ruling 4 and 5). Should `card.NeedsRoutes` be computed in Go before `unworded` and carried as a field, so the candidate set can include it? That is the only way defect 4 is fixable without re-reading briefs.
- `run/serve.go:143` reads the roster at `max(1, FleetWidthCap)`; before `seed` succeeds (129-137) the cap is 1. If the active version fails at seed and the built-in answers, the cap is the built-in's 8 regardless of what the active would say. Is a first roster read at width 1 or 8 acceptable, or should seed refuse to start the loop until the active version has answered once?
- Round 5 says the size is over the 1.5k budget and asks Glenn to raise it or drop a feature. After defects 1, 2 and 6 and the duplicate validation in steps, about 250 lines come out without dropping a guarantee; is that the answer, or is the diff verb the one to drop?
