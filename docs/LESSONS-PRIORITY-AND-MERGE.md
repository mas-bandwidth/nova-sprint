## 1. Deal order and auto-balance

Order of kinds of work
- D1. The order is LAND > FIX > READ > WORK. Glenn 2026-10-18: "highest priority is landing. above everything else!" because landing frees work and unblocks dependencies. (topic-sprint-dealing-and-cards §finish-before-starting)
- D2. Why reads and fixes sit above work: "if reading, fixing and landing are all higher priority than work, the system balances. if the same, or lower, then the system backs up with more work coming in until it collapses." Glenn 2026-10-18. Rule of thumb: backlog in review or merging means stop feeding new work and drain. (same §)
- D3. The card ladder, highest first: `blocker`, `critical`, `fix`, `high`, `reader`, `normal` (default), `low` (fills only an idle lane). Glenn 2026-10-06, with 270 cards in review, 76 working and readers near idle: "I am now convinced that reads need to become a type of card priority." "This will help balance reads vs. work from now on." (SPEC §1 "Priority: reads are a card priority")
- D4. `blocker` = "this is the most important thing to do right now, stop everything, do this instead"; `critical` = "I need to do this right now". (SPEC §1 same)
- D5. Where a level comes from: a brief's `PRIORITY: <level>` header seeds it at `add`, else the stream default, then the `priority` verb (a `--reason` is required, as `drop`'s is). A misspelled level is refused. A read card's level is never set by hand; `priority` on a read or work card is refused and points at the primary. (SPEC §1 same)
- D6. `critical` is also computed: a card with 10 or more cards transitively behind it through needs (`CriticalBehind`, weight) is `critical` unless a level is set. It is shown as "critical (by weight, not yet ordered)" and orders nothing yet; ordering by weight and preemption by a blocker are "owed with the model". Its effect that does exist: a critical card starts on at least a pro route and is never dealt below it. Glenn 2026-10-04: "these critical blockers should have some elevated priority ... they should be at front of queue" (one night the root of about 140 schema cards was dealt like any other card). (SPEC §1 same; SPEC §7 "Weight")
- D7. A read inherits the higher of `reader` and its primary's level, so the reads of a blocker/critical/fix/high primary go to the front of the read queue. Glenn 2026-10-06: "that work stream jumps to the front of the reader and merge queue"; "work in review queues should be distributed according to priority too". (SPEC §1)
- D8. The deal goes down the ladder level by level in one plan: at each level a friend is asked the reads of that level first, then dealt work of that level in the room the reads leave; no work card of a level is dealt to a friend while a read of that level or above she may take waits. A fleet reader's room is its own, never a work lane. Reads and work share one width. (SPEC §1)
- D9. Rework priority: `set --rework-priority fix|high|keep` (default `fix`) is the level a normal or low primary gets when its next attempt opens, for failed work, broken reads, conflicts, harness faults, `rework`, and brief-defect corrections. Existing high/fix/critical/blocker stay. The primary, its new work and its later reads carry it. (SPEC §1; PLAYBOOK §8)
- D10. How a fix returns to the head of the queue: a score is given once at admission, copied to work cards, read cards and the merge place, and no move changes it, so a reworked primary sits ahead of primaries admitted after it. Only `rank` (the coordinator, receipted) changes a score. A friend's lane takes its dealt set in the deal's order (ladder, stream turns, work order), not dealt order. (SPEC §4 "Order")
- D11. Order within a level is the modelled one: stream turns for the deal and the ask, work order for the friends' ask (tla/SprintTables.tla). Weight within a level is owed. (SPEC §1)
- D12. Equal ordering is not enough: at one level a read goes before work, so a high primary's read comes before high work; `reader` reads sit between high and normal work. (SPEC §1)
- D13. Strict deal: the deal refuses new work while a fix waits on the same row, so fixes are taken first and workers retry a refused take. (topic-sprint-reads-and-reports §rowan-row-ok-pct-honest-finish, 2026-10-17)

Reads are dealt at once, in parallel, to distinct readers
- D14. The deal deals every read a primary in review still needs at once, before any work card of the same deal (SPEC §6 "The deal deals the reads, first"; it notes "placing the reads within the ladder ... is owed"). ReadsNeeded is one for a flash card, two for pro or heavy, or `set --reads`.
- D15. A read card is `<primary>.r<attempt>.<reader>`, goes to a different unit each, never the unit that worked the attempt, never a unit that already read that attempt; a read waits for room and nothing else (no judgment, no backoff). `cannot ask` is only for a read no unit may ever read by capability. Glenn 2026-10-06: "I don't want any serial reads again." Glenn 2026-10-18: "if we have serial reads again, I'm going to be really sad." (SPEC §6 "Who reads"; topic-sprint-design-rules §sprint-needs-a-normative-spec)
- D16. Read cost order: a unit with a free lane before one with none (a lane counts the queue), then nearest served tier at or above the read's (never below), then a persisted round-robin cursor `read_deal_index_<tier>`. Glenn 2026-10-11: "Deal logic should be using ((index++) % n) to roll around". A read holds half a slot of its unit's width. (SPEC §6 "Who reads", "Half a slot")
- D17. Read counts over time (all Glenn): 2026-10-20 "2 friends, 2 fleet" then "increase reads to 5" (2 friends + 3 fleet, distinct fleet models); same day "drop to 3" (friends=2, fleet=1) to clear the review backlog; later 2026-10-20 standing rule, one friend read (friends=1, fleet=0), BROKEN only for a real defect ("Land shit!"). (topic-sprint-reads-and-reports §four-reads-two-friends-two-fleet; sprint-standing-rules)
- D18. The coordinator hand-reads only when the fleet cannot: 2026-10-16 Glenn ("Did you not consider that you can do these reads yourself?") with 83 in review; superseded 2026-10-19 ("This is silly. You can do heavy reads with ultramarine!"): check reader tiers and the Ultramarine route first, hand-read only own-row work or urgent rocketnet. (topic-sprint-reads-and-reports §coordinator-reads-when-reads-back-up)
- D19. A backup is named: `BackupOf` = `reads` while review exceeds working, `merges` while merging exceeds review plus working, else `none`; `where` prints it with the reads waiting. Four alarms (`alarm_review`, `alarm_merging`, `alarm_ready`, `alarm_fleet`) are one-judgment episodes pushed at start and at end. (SPEC §1 "backup state"; SPEC §8 "Backlog alarms")

Auto-balance: ready, working, deal-ahead
- D20. Ready is a one-tick state. Glenn 2026-10-19: "words have meaning. Ready is only a temporary state (1 second max) before they are distributed to workers on the next tick." A card that cannot start belongs in waiting. (topic-sprint-dealing-and-cards §ready-is-a-one-second-state)
- D21. Ready cards go to per-friend and per-fleet-machine ready queues even when the row is full; width caps what is worked, not what is queued. Glenn 2026-10-18: "That's what the ready queues are FOR". DealAhead is 2: a row holds up to 2 x width, ready plus working, reads included. (topic-sprint-dealing-and-cards §Ready cards go to ready queues; SPEC §5, §1)
- D22. The tick fills idle lanes first, by tier, every tick: eligibility by tier never class; among eligible friends, one with an idle lane before any with none, then most room, then name; a friend at DealAhead x width is never dealt. Glenn 2026-10-05: "You should automatically rebalance queues ... it should just happen mechanically." (SPEC §1 "friend-deal-idle-lanes-first")
- D23. The rebalance is its own part of the tick after the deal, across friends and fleet together: a queued, unstarted, unpinned card beyond a unit's free halves moves to a unit with a free lane that serves its tier (own tier first, then one tier up as overflow, never more, never below), at most once a tick, bounded by free lanes, never back to a row it left (`friends_left`, `rebalanced_from`), never a started card, a hard pin, a held stream's card, or a card whose route rests. Glenn 2026-10-06: "This should be a holistic rebalance ... BOTH"; 2026-10-11: "cards only may accrue per-fleet or friend, if there are not other machines/friends ready to do that work right now!". Reads rebalance in the same tick and all at once: "Not one card at a time per-tick." (SPEC §1 "The rebalance")
- D24. The friend level runs inside every tick after the deal, at most `FriendLevelPerTick` (4) cards per tick (the verb has no bound). "Started" is the store's own data (last beat names the card running, or a progress stamp). (SPEC §1)
- D25. One lane per card: a ready card that any friend's last beat names running is placed on no row. (SPEC §1 "one-lane-per-card")
- D26. Waiting promotes to ready on its own when needs land, one way only; the merge step that lands a card moves every waiting primary whose needs have all landed to ready, in the same step. Glenn 2026-09-25: "waiting -> ready should be a one way movement." (waiting-to-ready-one-way-ready-empties-at-once, ROWAN-NEW; SPEC §7)
- D27. Ready cards are taken mechanically, never by a session noticing: Glenn 2026-10-16 "again. this is not a one off thing rowan" and 2026-10-19 "This has to get fixed! I can't keep pestering on this!". (topic-sprint-dealing-and-cards §take-the-ready-queue-mechanically)
- D28. Cycle time is recorded per stage on the card (`admitted`, `ready_at`, `dealt_at`, `taken_at`, `finished_at`, `rework_s`, `read_asked_at`, `read_done_at`, `accepted`, `landed`) with median and p90 per stage. Glenn 2026-10-04: the wall-clock lens. (SPEC §1 "cycle-time-breakdownb")

Needs, holds, sentinels, blocked
- D29. Hard `needs` block dealing; soft `LAND-AFTER` orders only among cards both queued to merge and never blocks a deal. Add a need only when the card consumes the other's output; an overlap edge turned 151 of 466 edges into serial waits. Glenn 2026-10-18: "A work stream is never fully serial unless there is no other way." (SPEC §19 "Soft dependencies"; topic-sprint-dealing-and-cards §needs-are-true-dependencies-only; topic-sprint-design-rules §sprint-needs-a-normative-spec)
- D30. Priority only reorders cards already ready; a waiting card is moved by clearing its needs, not raising its priority. (PLAYBOOK §8; COORD §1)
- D31. A held sentinel blocks every card behind it and needs every card ahead of it; add it after the last parked card and rank the parked cards above its score, never at `--score 0`. Hold stream = nothing new dealt, in-flight cards finish. (topic-sprint-dealing-and-cards §held-sentinel-parks-everything-behind-it, 2026-10-17)
- D32. Priority orders work; stream holds are for real faults only. Glenn 2026-10-19: "unleash everything. priority will sort out rocketnet." (topic-sprint-dealing-and-cards §unleash-everything-priority-sorts)
- D33. `friend hold` / `hold` withdraws the friend's dealt and working cards, not only future deals; steer with `friend give` right after `add`. A `--dry-run` or explicit ids come before any bulk move. (topic-sprint-dealing-and-cards §never-hold-own-row-to-steer-deals; §dry-run-bulk-sprint-moves)
- D34. A HOLD finish strands the card in review; a CARRY alone never re-deals it; `rework` (or release/ack) the same turn. Set needs and tier before `rework`, because rework deals at once. (topic-sprint-dealing-and-cards §hold-needs-rework-not-just-carry; flaps-go-to-blocked-state)
- D35. Blocked is a card state. Glenn 2026-11-05: "if a card flaps around more than n times, it goes into blocked primary card state. blocked -> waiting -> ready -> etc."; "blocked card is colored black"; it "sits there until you review it"; entering blocked pushes `BLOCKED <card>` to the coordinator. Rows are never blocked. (flaps-go-to-blocked-state; sprint-hard-earned-pieces-carried-into-v2, Glenn 2026-11-06)
- D36. Do not confuse the two: v1's `blocker` is a priority level (D3, D4); `blocked` is a card state (D35). The v1 spec's "critical blockers" (2026-10-04) were cards with many dependents behind them (D6).
- D37. Stuck-card review runs mechanically every 10 minutes: waiting with every need landed, held by a judgment, merging but not queued, REVIEW OK not accepted. Glenn 2026-10-16: "This might be something you need to regularly do every 10 minutes". (topic-sprint-dealing-and-cards §review-stuck-cards-every-10-minutes)
- D38. Card quality decides dealing: card lint on `add`, `sprint check` before start (Glenn 2026-11-06: quality of cards "was a BIG PART of whether it went smoothly or was rough"). Cards are written pro-sized from the start; heavy only for design/model pieces (Glenn 2026-10-17 "Agreed!"). (sprint-hard-earned-pieces-carried-into-v2; topic-sprint-dealing-and-cards §card-pro-sized-from-the-start)
- D39. Brief edits on a working card bounce its next finish; on a card in review they retire its open reads and reopen the attempt; batch edits, message instead. (topic-sprint-dealing-and-cards §brief-edit-bounces-the-next-finish, 2026-10-15/18/24)

## 2. Merging wide

The shape
- M1. Three rules: (1) merge in work order, never random, the head of the stream's queued cell first; (2) in batches onto the sprint branch, the batch proved there; (3) a card's own failure lets the stream land on, a cause not the card's stops the stream and tells the coordinator, who may act on the card, the stream, or several streams together. (SPEC §7)
- M2. One branch per work stream. SPEC §7: "card heads land in a batch on their stream's branch sprint/<stream>, oldest first, one writer per stream; stream branches merge one at a time into dev (main where a repository has no dev); a card never lands on dev or main."
- M3. Work order = dependency order, then the card's created_at, oldest first; the foundation merges first, and dependents are never merged around it. Glenn 2026-09-25 1:05 PM ET: "You need to lock in this work stream based, order of working/dependency order merging. You must lock it in. YOU MUST NOT FORGET THIS AGAIN." (merge-per-stream-in-work-order-never-forget, ROWAN-NEW)
- M4. Never one card at a time: a batch is every ready member of one stream. Glenn 2026-09-25: "ALWAYS BATCH"; 3:45 PM: "Don't work one at a time. Work in a batch. Merge in work order to a branch per-work stream, go green, then merge that branch into dev. This is the 4th time I have had to say this." (one-stream-per-wave-never-serial-batches; merge-per-stream-in-work-order-never-forget, ROWAN-NEW)
- M5. A batch is never several streams in one branch, with no exception (pit stop, hand landing, cross-stream dependency). Glenn 2026-09-25 6:05 PM: "No. One branch *per-work stream*. This is the 6th time I have told you this." The order between streams is dependency order; each stream is still its own branch, merged once. (merge-per-stream-in-work-order-never-forget)
- M6. `--batch n` = first n queued cards by score. A stuck card is a barrier: the merge step never passes an earlier stuck or queued card; a later card never passes an unfinished predecessor (waiting, ready, working or review), and the blocker names the predecessor. Stella's finding, relayed 2026-09-26. (SPEC §7; merge-per-stream-in-work-order-never-forget)

Wide across streams
- M7. Merges across streams run in parallel; only the final landing is serial. Glenn 2026-10-07: "We can do merges across work streams in parallel. The only thing that needs to be serial is the merge after." `--land-parallel` default 4; each stream has its own worktree of the repository's clone; the batch's tree is gated once as a whole (not per head), and each head is gated alone only if that gate is red, to blame the red head. Landing is one lane per (clone, base), in priority order within a base; different clones/bases land side by side. (SPEC §7 "The pass"; tla/LandPass.tla)
- M8. The land order across streams: a stream's level is the highest level of any card in its merging set (never its default, never its oldest card), then the stream with the most cards behind any merging card, then stream order; inside a stream oldest first. Glenn 2026-10-06: "a normal stream with one critical card merging goes ahead of a high stream whose merging cards are all high". (SPEC §1 "The landing order")
- M9. Waits are bounded: a batch that waits for a width slot, a same-base turn or another stream's gate past `LandDeadline` is refused for this pass without blaming a card, so a ready stream behind k stuck ones waits at most k x LandDeadline. (SPEC §7 "The pass")
- M10. The first pass of 2026-10-07 ran sixteen gates one after another for twenty minutes; the new pass runs eight, up to four at once, and a base gate once. (SPEC §7)
- M11. Non-priority languages merge into their own integration branches (`lang/<language>`), one writer per branch; priority work goes straight to main; later one PR per language to main. Glenn 2026-10-29: "just land the non-priority languages in their own branches and be patient." Do not cancel, rerun or reshuffle CI queues. (land-non-priority-in-own-branches-be-patient)
- M12. Batching inside a stream is separate from merging PRs into main: merge to main only when CI ran against the current main and is fully green; when main is red compare failing tests, never job names; when speed matters serialize and let each merge re-run CI; after each merge read `gh run list --branch main` before the next. Glenn 2026-10-27 "I want this wrapped up quickly" was the trigger. Benches are the platform gate; CI is the merge gate; both must be green. (merge-only-on-ci-against-current-main)
- M13. Inside the lander the same idea: a batch cut from a tip the base no longer has is re-merged onto the new tip and pushed without a new gate only when the files it changes and the files landed since are disjoint and the same heads merged; otherwise gated once on the combined tree. A red combined gate refuses the batch for this pass, records no fact, stops no stream, and the next pass tries again. (SPEC §7 "The pass")

Refusals, stops, reds
- M14. A card's own refusal (conflict in its own file at the base tip, E12 files outside PATHS, the lander's checks, its merged tree failing the tree gate) never stops the stream. The card leaves merge as `returned`; the stream stays merging so `land` lands its others in the same pass; the coordinator gets one happened note ("the landing refused a card's head: reworked at the tip") with nothing to answer. Files outside PATHS go to review for the `widen` rule. Glenn 2026-10-06: "There should be no manual step you need to remember to do. Just a notification." (SPEC §7 "A card's own refusal")
- M15. The same refusal twice, or exhausted widenings, is the brief's bound ("the brief is wrong, not the worker"); an accepted card in merging is never judged by the attempt cap alone. (SPEC §7; tla/Land.tla AcceptedNeverCapJudged)
- M16. A cause not the card's stops the stream: a ledger the lander could not resolve, a head origin does not hold, a red batch or red stream branch, a rejected push, the base failing its tree gate, a need of another stream's card, a base not on origin. Each stop and resume is an immutable happened notice, pushed to the seat as `STREAM STOPPED <stream> cause=<cause> waiting=<n> at=<time>` / `STREAM RESUMED ...` (kind=blocker), and a status line to the friends owning queued or stuck cards. (SPEC §7; SPEC §8)
- M17. `resume` is refused while a cause is unresolved, requires `--did` after a red stream branch, puts the card back at its queue head, and a cross-stream stop is answered by `rank <other> --first`, never by landing the other card around the order. (SPEC §7; PLAYBOOK §8)
- M18. Base red: a queued head that passes the tree gate lands first as the base cure; the base is re-gated after 2 and 5 minutes; the third failure stops the stream that met it with one judgment for the base; other streams that meet the same red are refused under that judgment and not stopped; a green tip resumes every stream stopped only on that red by rule. Never fast-forward the base by hand (the coordinator did on 2026-10-05). (PLAYBOOK §5; SPEC §8 "land-base-gate-stops-stream", "v11-base-red-auto-resume-now")
- M19. A conflict is a merge-step matter, not a new attempt. Glenn 2026-10-17: "i don't see why these bad merges should become fixes. they should be done in the merge step." Ledger-only conflicts are resolved by the lander (entry-by-entry for roadmap/evidence ledgers, regenerated for generated pages, class ledgers and keyed tables); a ledger conflict it cannot resolve stops the stream; a conflict in any other file is the card's, reworked at the tip, never resolved on the card's branch so no change a reader has not read lands. (topic-sprint-dealing-and-cards §write-cards-from-the-build; LANDING; SPEC §7)
- M20. Soft dependencies in a batch: `MergeSoftOrder` puts A ahead of B when both are queued; if A is not in merging the dependency is ignored for this batch (B lands, A re-merges later); a skipped card is named and the batch lands every other card; the stream is never refused whole for it. (SPEC §19)
- M21. The record is fact-checked: `merge --landed <id>@<head>` is refused unless each card is queued in the stream at exactly that head and the head is an ancestor of the fetched base tip (checked by the caller with `git merge-base --is-ancestor`); a bare `merge` on a real store is refused; a push the report missed is marked `pushed-unreported <sha>` and recorded by the next land before any new merge. (SPEC §7; "land-record-unreported-push-bc.w2")
- M22. The protected branches (dev, main) are never landed on except by the marked promotion stream; a card cut on dev is re-cut onto the stream branch at `add`. (found 2026-10-04: cards cut with `BASE: dev` restarted the promotion run at every landing.) (SPEC §7 "The sprint branch", "protected branches")
- M23. Stream branches reach dev in bursts: the tick raises "dev is behind" at 25 landed cards or the oldest landed 30 minutes ago. Glenn 2026-10-04: "We must merge into dev continually, at least in bursts"; "promote every cycle or drift causes a big fuckup" (105 conflicts and an evening). The "dev sync every cycle" step was never built; only the drift reader exists. (SPEC §7 "Dev is behind", "Dev sync every cycle")
- M24. The lander pauses (cards stay queued, nothing recorded, no stream stopped) while a coordinator merge window is open or the forge merge queue of the base holds a group; an unreadable queue pauses too. (SPEC §7 "The lander's pause")
- M25. Who merges and who tells main: Glenn 2026-10-20 "Have you merged into main yet? FFS rowan." and "yes, it's you're job!"; 2026-10-22 "it's not waiting on me. you do it." Merge landings into main with `--no-ff`, never force, tell Glenn the sha; never ask him to press merge. (main-is-my-job)
- M26. The merge table: columns `merge | queued | merged | stuck | ci | state`. `queued -> merged` happens only when a batch has landed; `merged` only grows and equals work landed; `stuck` holds cards that cannot merge for a cause not their own. The one text row sums queued, merged and stuck over every stream; `ci` and `state` show the value that most needs the coordinator's eye: ci `red`, then `green`, then `-`; state `stopped` (with a count, `stopped 1`), then `merging`, `waiting`, `landed`, `-`. The per-stream rows stay in `where --json`. Hidden columns: `returned` and `ctl` (state, cause, ci, since). Glenn 2026-10-01: "do the same for merge" (one summed row). (SPEC §1 "The tables")
- M27. Stream states: `waiting` (nothing queued), `merging` (batches moving), `stopped` (needs the coordinator; ci and the notification say why), `landed` (every primary landed). Every step that changes the queue keeps the state true; a stopped stream stays stopped until `resume`. (SPEC §7)
- M28. Observe merging: Glenn 2026-09-26: "you don't watch (as coordinator) and see when something is stuck or taking too long, or going one at a time"; "I don't trust you to just hand it off and it gets done." A landing is never handed off and left; a stream head that has not moved in ten minutes is an event to act on. (merge-per-stream-in-work-order-never-forget; v1's mechanical forms: alarm_merging, `STREAM STOPPED` push, stuck-card review D37)

## 3. What went wrong in v1, with the v2 invariant

Reads starving and queues backing up
- W1. Reads were not a priority, so review fell behind work: 270 cards in review, 76 working, readers near idle (2026-10-06). v2 invariant: reads outrank work in the same deal and a read waits for room only (D3, D8, D14, D15).
- W2. Priority equal to or below work: "the system backs up with more work coming in until it collapses" (2026-10-18). v2 invariant: land, fix and read each rank above new work (D1, D2).
- W3. 83 cards in review with 81 reads waiting while the coordinator did not hand-read (2026-10-16); 41 reads stuck (2026-10-17); heavy reviews "cannot ask" because no heavy route or reader tier existed (2026-10-19). v2 invariant: a read exists only where a reader serves its tier, and a missing tier is a named configuration judgment (T5, D18).
- W4. Serial reads ("I don't want any serial reads again" 2026-10-06). v2 invariant: all needed reads dealt in one tick to distinct readers (D14, D15).
- W5. 16 to 19 heavy cards sat undealt in the pool while friend rows were at width (2026-10-18); 2 ready cards sat in a held stream (2026-10-19); 14 cards sat in ready "for no reason" (2026-09-25). v2 invariant: ready lasts one tick; a card that cannot start is waiting (D20, D21).
- W6. The fleet sat at 7 of 132 slots with 0 ready because nearly every card was heavy (2026-10-17), and Emma sat idle with 16 slots because her row listed only frontier and heavy (2026-10-20). v2 invariant: cards are pro-sized, and every row's tiers cover the cards that exist (D38, T14).
- W7. A card oscillated waiting <-> ready every tick for an hour (2026-09-25). v2 invariant: waiting -> ready is one way (D26).

Holds, flaps and invisible stalls
- W8. Flaps: six primaries re-dealt 280 to 1,150 times over three hours and five cards with about 48 environment exits each, noticed only when Glenn saw the dashboard (2026-11-05: "I want this flapping to never happen again, and not be noticed by you"). v2 invariant: a producer that bounces more than n times goes to `blocked`, pushed to the coordinator; two problems, two fixes (D35). The causes recorded the same day: rework before needs were set, staffing cards whose needs had not landed, tier changed after a deal. Value of n: no source found.
- W9. Two held sentinels added at `--score 0` put 55 cards at the front and each gated about 90 others; the fleet sat idle with 0 ready (2026-10-17). v2 invariant: a sentinel parks only cards behind it (D31).
- W10. How a card declared its tier
- T1. Four tiers, ladder `flash < pro < heavy < frontier`. A card's tier is its brief's line 1 (`tier: <t>`), or the tier the coordinator pinned: `rework --tier`, `brief --tier`, `nova-sprint tier <card> --set <t> --reason <text>`. - T2. The card's own field is authoritative: `nova-sprint card <id>` prints `tier=<t> ceiling=<t>`; the line-1 text goes stale after `recut --tier`. (respect-the-card-tier in topic-tiers-models-and-budget, found 2026-10-16)
- T3. Tier is set by contents and is mechanical. Glenn 2026-10-16: "You decide on the tier for a card based on its contents. You deal that card out according to its tier. The work is done according to the models specified for that tier in nova-config. There is no ambiguity anywhere!!!" A tier-to-model helper must be code that reads nova-config. (topic-tiers-models-and-budget §respect-the-card-tier)
- T4. Content rules in `add`: a card that writes a new TLA+ model is admitted at `heavy` at least (refused below); a card that only edits what `tla/` holds is any tier. Glenn 2026-10-04 (gate wall); the owner's cost rule. (SPEC §5 "gate's wall", `sprint.ModelTier`)

How a route or row declared the tiers it serves
- T6. A route is a nova-config `route` row: a tier, a provider and model (one model each), a budget, a deadline, enabled. A tier's route array is a nova-config `tier` row: an ordered list of route names, a name repeated for more turns; a tier with none takes its enabled routes in name order. Provider rows (`providers`) declare billing and funding and ride the same read. Edits go through `nova-config route|tier|provider set` and `apply`. (SPEC §5; COORD §5)
- T7. A friend's row lists the tiers she may take (`nova-config friend set <f> --tiers ...`); a friend whose row names no tier takes none. ## 5. Rules for v2

Policy layer: deal order
- R1. Rank kinds of work LAND > FIX > READ > WORK; landing, fixing and reading must each outrank new work or the system backs up (D1, D2).
- R2. Provide the seven-level card priority (`blocker`, `critical`, `fix`, `high`, `reader`, `normal`, `low`), seeded by brief header, then stream default, then a reasoned verb; refuse unknown levels; never settable on a read card (D3 to D5).
- R3. A read inherits its primary's level when higher; a reworked attempt reopens at `fix` (or per the rework-priority setting); the score is given once at admission and only `rank` changes it (D7, D9, D10).
- R4. Deal level by level, reads before work at the same level, and no work card at a level while a read at that level or above is waiting for that unit (D8, D12).
- R5. Deal all reads a card needs at once to distinct readers, none serial, none the worker, each waiting only for room (D14 to D16).
- R6. Fill idle lanes first by tier on every tick, hold DealAhead (2) x width per row with width capping work and not the queue, rebalance queued unstarted cards across friends and fleet each tick, and never move a started, pinned or held-stream card or return a card to a row it left (D21 to D25).
- R7. Ready lasts one tick; waiting -> ready is one way and automatic; the landing step releases the waiting cards it unblocks in the same step (D20, D26).
- R8. Hard needs block dealing, soft `after` orders landing only, sentinels park only what is behind them, and holds change future dealing and report what they withdraw (D29, D31, D33).
- R9. A card that flaps more than n times goes to `blocked`, pushed to the coordinator and left until the coordinator unblocks it; the value of n has no source found (D35, W8).
- R10. Name the backup (`reads`, `merges`, `none`) and push alarms as episodes: review above threshold, merging above threshold, nothing ready while cards wait, fleet below percent (D19).
- R11. Card quality gates dealing: lint at `add`, `check` before start, pro-sized cards by default (D38).
- R12. A HOLD finish raises a judgment and a rework verb re-deals; a brief edit never retires a reviewed head's reads (D34, D39).
- R13. Critical-by-weight (10 or more behind) orders the deal and the ask in v2; v1 never ordered by weight ("owed with the model") so the v2 order has no v1 behaviour to copy, only the 10-card threshold and the "front of queue" intent (D6).

Landing step: merge wide
- R14. Land per work stream: one branch per stream, one writer, members in dependency order then created_at, all ready members in one batch, then that branch merges into the trunk (M2 to M5).
- R15. Never land a card alone, never mix streams in one branch, never pass an earlier stuck or queued card, never pass an unfinished predecessor (M5, M6).
- R16. Merge streams in parallel (default width 4, one worktree each), gate each batch once, land serially per (clone, base) in stream-priority order (highest card level in the merging set, then most behind, then stream order), and bound every wait with a deadline that refuses without blaming a card (M7 to M9).
- R17. Merge into the base only on a check run against the current base tip; a base that moved forces a fresh check unless the changed files are disjoint and the same heads merged; after each merge, read the base's CI before the next (M12, M13).
- R18. A card's own refusal returns that card and lets the stream land on, with a notification to the coordinator and no manual step; the same refusal twice is a brief bound (M14, M15).
- R19. A cause not the card's stops the stream and pushes `STREAM STOPPED <stream> cause waiting at` to the coordinator, and a resume pushes `STREAM RESUMED`; resume is refused while the cause stands (M16, M17).
- R20. Base red: a head that passes the gate lands first, re-gate after 2 and 5 minutes, third failure stops only the stream that met it with one judgment for the base, green tip resumes by rule (M18).
- R21. A conflict is a landing-step matter: ledgers resolved by the lander, other conflicts reworked at the tip and never resolved on the card's branch (M19).
- R22. Record landings only with each card's head verified an ancestor of the pushed base tip; recover a push that was not recorded before any new merge (M21).
- R23. Keep the merge table `queued / merged / stuck / ci / state` with the four stream states, the summing and worst-first display rules, and hidden `returned` and `ctl` columns; merging must be visible without the coordinator asking (M26, M27, M28, W17).
- R24. Promote stream branches to the protected branch in bursts and never land on a protected branch directly (M22, M23); pause landing under a merge window or a held forge queue (M24).
- R25. Non-priority streams may land into their own integration branches and wait their turn; do not churn CI queues (M11).

Tiers and routes
- R26. Support four tiers ordered `flash < pro < heavy < frontier`; a card declares its tier on its brief and the coordinator may pin or raise it with a reason; the card's field is authoritative (T1, T2).
- R27. The tier of a card is chosen by its contents and the model for the tier comes from config read by code, never chosen by hand (T3).
- R28. Content rules fix a minimum tier: new formal model at heavy or above, critical at pro or above, a gate measured over the flash bound at pro (T4).
- R29. Routes are config rows (tier, provider, model, budget, deadline, enabled); a tier's ordered route array with repeated names for extra turns is a separate config row; friends and readers list the tiers they serve (T6, T7).
- R30. One check, `tierServed`, answers whether a tier is served by an enabled funded route or an up, un-held friend whose row lists it; every verb that validates a tier uses it (T11).
- R31. Never deal, work or read a card below its tier and never fall back; when no route serves a tier, raise one named judgment per tier and keep the card ready (T12, T13).
- R32. Draw routes round-robin per tier, advanced once per card dealt; a pin and a `first` flag are honoured (T14).
- R33. A card is at its tier from its first deal; the only automatic escalation is a friend's capped lane re-dealt once at the next tier up on the ladder, a second cap or a pinned tier being failed work; the coordinator may raise a card with a reason (T17 to T19).
- R34. A route rests on transient provider failures (3 of the last 10, 30 minutes) or by the coordinator's word, never on a no-result take; a rested route is not drawn from and its cards are withdrawn (T20, T21).
- R35. Provider funding is a config fact that flips to unfunded on a billing failure and only the owner restores; an out-of-funds provider rests all its routes, and all providers out stops the sprint (T22).
- R36. Config check fails on a funded route in no tier array, a tier with no served route, or missing provider rows, and a config write must be checked as applied (T23, T28).
- R37. Rows are never narrowed to steer deals, and cards are pro-sized so that every tier's capacity is used (T31, D38).
- R38. Record route, model, tier and cost on every attempt (SPEC §5 `ATTEMPT` line).
